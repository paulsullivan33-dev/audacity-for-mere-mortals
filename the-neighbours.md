# The Neighbours

What else does this job, and what each
choice trades. Free and open source
unless noted.

## OBS Studio

Records and streams audio and video
*together*, live. If your problem is
"capture it as it happens" — a stream,
a lesson, a screen with narration —
OBS is the tool, and its recordings
then come *here* for their clean-up
(this guide's companion volume covers
it). It does not edit; Audacity does
not stream. The pair cover a household
studio between them.

## Ardour

A full digital audio workstation:
multitrack recording, MIDI,
instruments, mixing desks, plug-in
galore. When a project outgrows
Audacity — a band, a scored podcast
with music you compose — Ardour is the
open-source destination, and your
Audacity habits (tracks, clips,
envelopes) transfer with translation.
It asks for a workstation's commitment
in return.

## Reaper

The other full DAW, commercial but
famously inexpensive, cross-platform,
and bottomless. Chosen by many
podcasters who outgrew Audacity but
not their budgets. Editing in Reaper is
non-destructive at every level, which
is its real argument; Audacity's
argument is that you already know
where everything is.

## Tenacity

A fork of Audacity from the period
when the project's stewardship was in
dispute. Largely compatible in spirit
and format family; smaller community.
Most mortals can stay on Audacity; the
fork exists so the option exists, and
knowing the name is enough here.

## Ocenaudio

A simpler, friendlier editor: one
track at a time, lovely spectral view,
fast for trimming a single file and
seeing inside it. The tool for "open
this WAV, cut the ends, tell me what
that buzz is" when Audacity feels like
starting a project to slice a
cucumber.

## SoX and ffmpeg

The command-line sound processors.
SoX generates, converts and *measures*
(the lab's every number came from its
`stat`), ffmpeg converts anything to
anything and owns the video-adjacent
work. They compose into scripts the
scripting pipe (chapter 10) only
gestures at. Their trade is the
obvious one: no waveform, no undo, no
mercy — everything is a command you
can re-read, which is also their
defence.

## The short version

- Record and fix sound you already
  have, on a screen, by eye: **here**.
- Record it live, as it happens:
  **OBS Studio**.
- Compose, perform, produce: **Ardour**
  or **Reaper**.
- One file, one quick trim:
  **Ocenaudio**.
- A thousand files, no screen: **SoX /
  ffmpeg**.
